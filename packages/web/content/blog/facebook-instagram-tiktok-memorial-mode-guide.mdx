## The Message Nobody Expects

Three weeks after Laura's husband died suddenly at 41, she noticed something strange. His Facebook account — still active, unmanaged — started recommending him to mutual friends under "People You May Know." His Instagram profile received a birthday reminder notification from the platform. She didn't know how to get into his accounts. She hadn't even known you could prepare for this.

That's how most people find out: too late, and in grief.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: **every major social media platform has a death policy**, and most of them let you set things up *in advance*. If you're living in the UK, the Netherlands, Germany, or anywhere in Europe, your accounts will outlive you whether you plan for it or not. The only question is whether that happens on your terms or someone else's.

This guide tells you exactly what to do — platform by platform — right now, while you still can.

## Facebook: Legacy Contact + Memorialization Request

Facebook has the most mature death-management system of any major platform. You have two main options:

### Option 1: Appoint a Legacy Contact

A Legacy Contact is someone you designate to manage your memorial profile after you die. They can:

- Pin a tribute post to your profile
- Respond to new friend requests
- Update your profile picture and cover photo

What they **cannot** do:
- Log into your account
- Read your private messages
- Remove friends or see your activity log

**How to set this up right now:**
1. Go to **Settings → Memorialization Settings**
2. Under "Legacy Contact," choose someone you trust
3. Decide whether to allow them to download a copy of what you've shared

### Option 2: Request Account Deletion After Death

If you'd prefer your account simply vanish, you can set that preference now. Facebook will delete the account once a verified death report is received. This is irreversible — there's no recovery.

### What Happens If You Do Nothing?

Facebook will memorialize your account automatically once they receive a report from someone. The profile gets an "In Remembrance" banner but no one can manage it or log in. It just… exists, awkwardly, in perpetuity. Friends get birthday reminders. The algorithm sometimes surfaces your content to people who didn't know you'd died.

**The action: Go to Facebook Settings → Memorialization Settings — do it today.**

## Instagram: Legacy Contact (Yes, It Exists)

Instagram added Legacy Contact support in 2023, but almost nobody knows about it. It works similarly to Facebook's system, since Meta owns both platforms.

### Setting Up Your Instagram Legacy Contact

1. Open the Instagram app
2. Go to **Settings → Account → Memorialization Settings**
3. Enter the username of your chosen legacy contact
4. They'll receive a notification

A Legacy Contact on Instagram can:
- Request memorialization of your account after you die
- Manage the memorialized account within certain limits

They **cannot** log in as you, read messages, or see content you didn't share publicly.

### What About Your DMs?

Private messages on Instagram die with you — they cannot be accessed by family members, legacy contacts, or executors. This is actually a privacy protection, but it also means any important conversations, business arrangements, or sentimental messages in your inbox are permanently lost.

**If Instagram contains anything important — save it now.**

### What Happens If You Do Nothing?

Instagram will freeze your account in its current state once it's memorialized. Your photos stay up. Stories are gone. Your followers still see your profile. The algorithm stops promoting your posts. But no one can manage, update, or remove the account without a legacy contact or formal legal process — which varies by country and can take months.

## TikTok: The Platform That Hasn't Quite Figured This Out Yet

TikTok's death policy is, frankly, underdeveloped compared to Meta's. As of 2026, TikTok does **not** have a formal legacy contact feature.

### What TikTok Does Offer

TikTok will memorialize or remove accounts upon request from a verified family member or legal representative. To make this request, you need:

- Proof of your relationship to the deceased (birth certificate, marriage certificate)
- Proof of death (death certificate)
- The deceased's TikTok username

Submit via TikTok's **Help Center → Account Recovery → Deceased User Account** flow.

### The Problem With Doing Nothing

TikTok accounts without activity eventually become targets for account hijackers who prey on dormant accounts. Your profile, videos, and followers are all vulnerable if the account isn't secured. Unlike Facebook or Instagram, TikTok has no automatic memorialization trigger.

### What You Can Do Right Now

Since TikTok lacks a legacy contact option:

1. **Write your username and password somewhere secure** — a password manager, a sealed letter, or a digital vault like LegacyShield
2. **Document which email and phone number** are linked to the account (recovery depends on these)
3. **Name someone in your will or digital directive** who should handle your TikTok account

If your TikTok account has a significant following or generates income (TikTok Creator Fund, brand partnerships), treat it as a business asset in your estate plan.

## The Cross-Platform Problem Nobody Talks About

Here's what makes this genuinely difficult: **your social media accounts are not legally your property**. You own a license to use the platform, not the account itself. This means:

- You cannot "inherit" a social media account the way you inherit a bank account
- Platform terms of service govern what happens — not your will
- Different EU countries have different laws about digital assets

For European residents, this is particularly relevant. Germany's *Bundesgerichtshof* ruled in 2018 that heirs can access a deceased person's Facebook account (case III ZR 183/17). Dutch law is less settled. French and Spanish law treat digital accounts inconsistently.

**The safest approach: prepare in-platform settings + document your accounts in writing.**

## Your 20-Minute Action Plan

You don't need to do everything today. But you should do *something* today.

**Priority 1 (5 minutes):**
- Go to Facebook Settings → Memorialization Settings

**Priority 2 (5 minutes):**

- Add a Legacy Contact

**Priority 3 (10 minutes):**
- Write down your TikTok username, linked email, and phone number
- Store this in a secure location your family can access

And beyond that: **think about what you want people to find when they look you up after you're gone.** Your profiles will keep existing. Make sure they exist the way you want them to.
